I’m Dominic Varghese, a computational materials science Ph.D. candidate at the University of Minnesota.

I combine first-principles simulations, physics-informed machine learning, and high-performance computing to understand materials and accelerate their discovery. My research connects oxide interfaces, lattice dynamics, and machine-learning interatomic potentials.

01

Dec 2023 — Present

Modeling the flexoelectric effect in oxides with Prof. Turan Birol.

CEMS, University of Minnesota ↗

  • Build DFT datasets with VASP and ABINIT, fine-tune equivariant GNN interatomic potentials (MACE, NequIP, PET), and run simulations on GPU-accelerated HPC clusters.
  • Study how epitaxial strain enhances flexoelectric response through optical phonon softening using DFPT, Landau theory, and group theory.
02

Mar 2025 — Present

Physics-informed ML for phonon prediction

CEMS, University of Minnesota ↗

Predicting lattice dynamics with Prof. Turan Birol.

  • Predict high-dimensional dynamical matrices from nearest-neighbor force-constant features to infer phonon dispersions under strain and pressure.
  • Validate predictions against neutron-scattering experiments and capture strain-induced acoustic branch softening.
03

Aug 2022 — Jun 2023

Undergraduate Researcher

SSCU, Indian Institute of Science ↗

Research on the non-linear Hall effect in Ferroelectrics under the guidance of Prof. Awadhesh Narayan.

  • Identified optimal doping concentrations in ferroelectric perovskites that maximize the Berry Curvature Dipole (BCD), leading to an enhancement of the Non-Linear Hall effect.
  • Systematically varied doping concentrations and computed BCD using Quantum Espresso and Wannier90, successfully demonstrating a tunable electronic response.

Piezoelectric Modulus Prediction Using Machine Learning

Developed a complete machine learning pipeline to classify and predict the piezoelectric modulus for a large set of inorganic materials from the JARVIS database.

  • Trained on 1,354 inorganic materials from JARVIS; reached 78% classification accuracy.
  • Used crystal symmetry features and transfer learning to achieve a regression RMSE of 0.49.
